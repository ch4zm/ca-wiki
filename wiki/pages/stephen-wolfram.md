# Stephen Wolfram

## Description

Stephen Wolfram wrote "Universality and complexity in cellular automata" (1984) at the
Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, with support from the Office of Naval
Research.[^1] He ran its surveys with his own C program "CA", an interactive cellular
automaton simulator for the Sun workstation and VAX, which he presented at the
Interdisciplinary Workshop on Cellular Automata at Los Alamos in March 1983.[^2]

**Contributions in the wiki's sources.**

- The [[elementary-cellular-automaton](pages/elementary-cellular-automaton.md)] family and
  its rule numbering, the 32 "legal" rules, and the split into simple and complex rules
  (1983).[^3]
- Exact results for the [[additive-cellular-automaton](pages/additive-cellular-automaton.md)]
  rules, self-similar growth from seeds ([[fractal-dimension](pages/fractal-dimension.md)]),
  and two universality classes of statistics from random starts
  ([[self-organization](pages/self-organization.md)]) (1983).[^3]
- The ensemble view of [[irreversibility-and-entropy](pages/irreversibility-and-entropy.md)]:
  trajectories merge, entropy falls, and most configurations become unreachable
  (1983).[^3]
- The four [[wolfram-classes](pages/wolfram-classes.md)], with class 4 conjectured to be
  capable of universal computation and therefore unpredictable (1984).[^4]
- [[set-and-measure-entropy](pages/set-and-measure-entropy.md)], dimensions and
  propagation speeds as measures of order and chaos, with the names "set" and "measure"
  introduced to tidy up the terminology (1984).[^5]
- Code numbers for [[totalistic-cellular-automaton](pages/totalistic-cellular-automaton.md)]
  rules, which he used as his main sample (1984).[^6]

**Stated approach.** The 1984 paper describes itself as "preliminary steps towards a
general theory of cellular automaton behaviour". It says it "includes many conjectures and
tentative results" and is not meant as "a rigorous mathematical treatment".[^7]

**Acknowledged discussions.** The 1984 paper thanks, among others, C. Bennett,
J. Crutchfield, D. Lind, O. Martin, A. Odlyzko, N. Packard, W. Thurston and T. Toffoli, and
J. Milnor in particular "for extensive discussions and suggestions".[^8]
